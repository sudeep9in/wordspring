Wordspring privacy policy

Effective date: 24 November 2026 · Applies to the Wordspring app for Android, iOS, Windows and the web.

In short: Wordspring has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and the developer runs no server that receives your data. Your documents, reading positions and word list stay on your device. When you look up a word with online extras turned on, the word (and a few key words from its sentence) is sent to public dictionary services to get pronunciation and more meanings. You can turn this off in Settings.

Who we are

Wordspring is made by Sudeep Verma (“we”, “the developer”). Questions about this policy: wordspring.app@gmail.com.

What stays on your device

The app stores the following only on your device, in the app’s private storage. We never receive it.

  • Copies of the documents you open (PDF, EPUB, Word, web pages, text) and where you stopped reading.
  • Your word list: each saved word, its meaning, the sentence and document it came from, and any AI explanation you saved.
  • How often you looked words up, your reading streak (the last day you read and how many days in a row), your settings, and a cache of earlier online lookups so they work offline.
  • AI tutor settings, including an API key if you enter one for your own AI server.

The built-in dictionary works entirely on your device. Documents you open from another app (“Open with” or “Share”) are copied into the app, and the temporary copy is deleted as soon as it has been read.

Your device’s own backup service (for example Google or iCloud backup) may include this app data, depending on your device settings. That backup is handled by your device provider under its privacy policy, not by us.

What is sent over the internet, and when

WhenWhat is sentTo whom
You look up a word and online extras are on (the default; Settings → Use online extras) The word or phrase you selected, its base form, and up to six key words from its sentence (used to pick the meaning that fits). Not the rest of your document. Free Dictionary API (dictionaryapi.dev), Datamuse API, and Wikipedia (Wikimedia Foundation) for names and phrases
You press the speaker button A request for the word’s pronunciation recording, when one is available; otherwise your device’s own text-to-speech voice is used The site hosting the recording (listed by the Free Dictionary API)
You open a web page in Wordspring A normal request for that page and its images, like a web browser The website you chose, and the sites hosting its images
You use the AI tutor (off by default in the phone apps) The words you selected, their sentence, about 600 characters of nearby text, the document’s title, the dictionary meanings, and any follow-up questions you type Only the AI server you set up in Settings → AI tutor. By default this is software on your own computer (Ollama or LM Studio), so nothing leaves your computer.

As with any internet request, these services can see your device’s IP address and basic technical details (such as the app or browser version). We have no agreement with them and receive nothing from them; their own privacy policies apply. Dictionary lookups use encrypted connections (HTTPS). Web pages and AI servers use whatever the site or server you chose supports.

With online extras turned off, word lookups send nothing. Reading web pages and the AI tutor only connect when you use them.

What we do not do

  • No account or sign-in, and we do not ask for your name, email or contact details.
  • No advertising, no analytics, no crash reporting, no tracking across apps or websites.
  • We do not sell or share your data, because we never receive it.

Permissions

The app uses internet access for the features above. It reads a document only when you open or share it with Wordspring. It does not use your location, contacts, camera, microphone or photos.

Children

Wordspring is a general reading app for students aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age.

Your choices and deleting your data

  • Turn off online extras in Settings to keep every word lookup on your device.
  • Remove documents from your library, or words from your word list, inside the app.
  • Delete everything at once with Settings → Privacy & data → Delete all my data.
  • Uninstalling the app, or clearing its data in your device settings, deletes everything it stored.

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete on our side. If you have questions, contact us at the address above.

Changes to this policy

If the app’s handling of data changes, we will update this page and its effective date before the change takes effect.
